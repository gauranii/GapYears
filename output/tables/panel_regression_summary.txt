Panel regression on the full 2000-2021 country-year data
(n countries: 183 | n country-years: 3949 )

This compares three ways of asking the same question, gap ~ life_expectancy +
health_exp_pct_gdp, not picking one as correct:
  1. Cross-sectional OLS on the 2021 snapshot alone (R/03_analysis.R's model)
  2. Pooled OLS across all 22 years (ignores that observations from the
     same country are not independent)
  3. Country fixed-effects (within-country variation only, i.e. does a rise in a
     country's own life expectancy or spending coincide with a change in its own gap)

== Hausman test: fixed vs. random effects ==

	Hausman Test

data:  gap ~ life_expectancy + health_exp_pct_gdp
chisq = 9.0314, df = 2, p-value = 0.01094
alternative hypothesis: one model is inconsistent

A significant Hausman test (p < 0.05) favors fixed effects: the random-effects
assumption, that a country's own unobserved characteristics are uncorrelated
with its life expectancy and spending, is rejected. That is the expected result
here -- a country's baseline health-system quality is almost certainly correlated
with both its life expectancy and its gap.
